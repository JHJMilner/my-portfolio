About

Turning complexity into clarity.

I'm a Principal UX Researcher with a decade of experience helping organisations understand their users, untangle ambiguous problems, and build things that actually work.

Background

The work I do, and why I do it.

At Emids Technologies, I lead qualitative and quantitative research programmes for enterprise healthcare clients across the United States — the kind of projects where the stakes are high, the stakeholders are many, and the research has to earn its seat at the table. My work spans the full research cycle, from early discovery and problem framing through to usability testing, roadmap input, and strategic synthesis.

Before specialising in healthcare, I worked across a wide range of sectors — government, non-profit, agriculture, retail, and education. That breadth taught me something important: the fundamentals of good research travel. Whether you're improving a clinical workflow or redesigning a consumer app, the core challenge is the same — help people make better decisions by giving them a clearer picture of reality.

I'm drawn to problems that don't have obvious answers. The ones where teams are stuck not because they lack ideas, but because they're working from different assumptions. Research, at its best, gives everyone in the room a shared foundation to work from. That's the work I find most satisfying.

Outside of work, I'm based in Ottawa — though I grew up in Winfield, Alberta, a small town that has since given me an enduring appreciation for wide open spaces and pragmatic problem-solving. When I'm not thinking about research, I'm usually reading, tending to an unreasonable number of houseplants, or working on some kind of craft project.

How I work

Research that moves things forward.

01

Evidence over assumption

Teams move faster when they agree on what's true. I build a shared evidence base that replaces gut-feel debates with grounded, defensible decisions — and I make sure that evidence is communicated in a way the whole organisation can act on.

02

Pragmatic, not precious

Good research fits the context. I match methods to the question, the timeline, and the resources available — not to an ideal process. A well-scoped study that lands on time is worth far more than a perfect one that arrives too late.

03

Insights that stick

Findings that sit in a slide deck change nothing. I focus as much on how research is shared as on how it's conducted — building narratives and facilitated conversations that help teams genuinely internalise what they've learned.
